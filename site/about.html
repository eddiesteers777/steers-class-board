About

Hi, I'm Eddie Steers.

I teach 7th and 8th grade math in Shelby County, Alabama, and I coach our middle school boys' soccer team. I built Anchor Board for my own classroom.

A note from me

To my students I'm Mr. Steers. I teach regular and accelerated math, and a lot of my students walk in still building the foundations everything else stands on: fractions, negative numbers, what an equation is really asking. My classes are sixty minutes long, and I want every one of those minutes to count for them.

For a long time I built slides every day for three different classes and clicked through them from the front of the room. It worked, but it kept me at the front of the room. The agenda lived in one place, the bellringer in another, the timer in a third, and every switch cost a minute and a little of the room's attention.

So I started building the board I wished I had. The agenda, the bellringer, the notes, the timers and the review games are all on one screen, and I run it from my Chromebook while I walk around and look at what students are actually writing. That part of teaching, sitting next to a kid while the idea clicks, is the part I never want to give up.

Everything on it gets tried in my own room first. If something slows a lesson down or leaves a student lost, it gets fixed or it gets cut. Worked examples show every step, because a student who sees why a step happens can do the next problem on their own.

I'm still learning, too. I'm working on a master's in secondary mathematics education at the University of Montevallo and a National Certificate for STEM Teaching. Both keep me asking how my students learn best, and the answers keep finding their way onto this board.

If you teach and any of this sounds like your classroom, I'd love to hear from you.

Eddie

What I care about

  • Calm. One screen, one job. Nothing covers the lesson.
  • Fast in class. If a tool makes a teacher stop and think mid-lesson, it gets cut.
  • Students first. No student accounts. Nothing about a student goes on the board unless the teacher puts it there.

Where it's going
